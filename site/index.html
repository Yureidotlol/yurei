幽霊
The legend

In the old stories, a yūrei is a soul that could not rest.

幽霊 means a faint spirit. In Japanese folklore it is the soul of someone who could not move on: a white burial robe, long dark hair, no feet, drifting at the edge of the lamplight.

What keeps a yūrei out is an ofuda, a strip of paper written in red ink and pressed to a door or a beam. The spirit can be anywhere. It cannot cross the seal.

Yurei is that idea, rebuilt on Robinhood Chain: something with real power that lives onchain, held by rules written in code instead of promises.

Abstract

Verify the answer. Don't recompute the mind.

Machine intelligence that acts with real money has two bad options today. It can run on a server you cannot inspect, which asks you to trust whoever runs it. Or it can run entirely inside smart contracts, which makes every step verifiable but costs orders of magnitude more than the answer is worth.

Yurei takes a third path, borrowed from how optimistic rollups secure their own state. A committed model runs off-chain. Each answer is posted onchain together with a cryptographic commitment to the computation that produced it. Anyone may challenge an answer within a fixed window. A dispute is narrowed, by bisection, to a single step of the model's arithmetic, and only that one step is re-executed onchain to decide who lied.

An honest answer costs one ordinary transaction. A dishonest one costs its author a bond. Around both sits the seal: a policy contract, fixed at creation, that decides what any answer is allowed to do, however persuasive the answer is.

証
The mechanism

From a question to an action, in five stages.

Each stage leaves a record on Robinhood Chain. Nothing moves until the record says it may.

  1. 01 / Request問

    The question is escrowed

    A requester submits a prompt with a fee and an upper bound on cost. The seal is consulted first: a request the policy would never allow to act is refused before any work is done.

    in prompt, fee, bounds
    out request id, escrow
  2. 02 / Inference考

    The spirit answers off-chain

    A bonded executor runs the committed model, whose weights are fixed by hash, in deterministic fixed-point arithmetic. It posts the answer and a Merkle root over the execution trace.

    in request, model hash
    out answer, trace root, bond
  3. 03 / Challenge疑

    Anyone may doubt it

    For a fixed window, any watcher who recomputes a different result can challenge by posting a bond of their own. Silence is the normal case, and silence is cheap.

    in answer, counter-claim
    out dispute, or nothing
  4. 04 / Proof証

    One step decides the truth

    The two parties bisect the trace until they disagree about a single operation. The chain re-executes that one operation, compares it to both claims, and slashes the bond of whoever was wrong.

    in disputed trace
    out one-step verdict, slashed bond
  5. 05 / Action封

    The seal releases, or holds

    After the window closes, the answer becomes final. If it calls for an action, such as a payment or a contract call, the seal checks it against limits written at creation. Inside them, it executes. Outside them, nothing happens.

    in final answer
    out action, or refusal

Trusted server

Cheap, opaque

Fast and inexpensive, but every answer is a claim you cannot check. The operator can change the model, the output or the rules without telling you.

Full onchain inference

Verifiable, impractical

Every multiplication runs in a contract. Correct by construction, but a short reply can cost more than most people would ever pay for it.

Yurei

Verifiable when it matters

Answers cost about one transaction. Proof is paid for only when someone disputes, and only for the single step in question.

疑
Open questions

What the research still has to answer.

A seal is only as strong as its weakest assumption. These are ours, stated plainly.

Determinism

Can two machines always agree?

Fraud proofs only work if honest executors produce identical traces. Quantized fixed-point arithmetic should make that true; it has to be shown across hardware and implementations.

Liveness

Who is watching?

An optimistic system is secure if at least one honest watcher checks each answer. Yurei needs watchers to be cheap to run and rewarded for catching lies.

Data availability

Where does the trace live?

Only the root is posted onchain. The full trace must stay retrievable for the length of the window, or a challenger cannot build a proof.

One-step cost

How expensive is the hardest step?

The single operation re-executed onchain must fit in one block. The model's layers are being shaped so that no step is too large to prove.
